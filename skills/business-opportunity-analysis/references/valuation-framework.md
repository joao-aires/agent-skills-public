# Valuation Framework

## Revenue multiples are outputs, not laws

A revenue multiple is a compressed expression of expectations about future cash generation, growth, quality, and strategic position.

Use multiples as scenario tools, not universal rules.

## Core conceptual model

```text
Company value
≈ current revenue base
× revenue quality
× growth expectations
× market / category position
× strategic premium
```

These are conceptual factors, not independently quoted multipliers.

## Main drivers of the multiple

### Growth rate

A company growing 20% annually should not normally receive the same multiple as one growing 150% annually.

Acceleration matters too. A business moving from €10M to €30M to €80M rapidly may be valued partly against expected forward revenue rather than trailing revenue.

### Gross margin

Higher gross margins mean more revenue can eventually convert into operating profit and free cash flow.

For AI products, check whether model / infrastructure costs materially reduce the economics.

### Retention and expansion

Strong NRR can make future revenue more predictable and reduce dependence on new-logo acquisition.

### Market size and runway

€30M ARR in a €300M market implies a very different runway from €30M ARR in a €30B market.

### Category position

Premiums can arise when a company appears likely to become:

- the default product
- an industry standard
- the dominant open-source project
- an ecosystem hub
- a strategic control point

### Capital efficiency

Growth purchased with extreme burn is generally less valuable than comparable growth achieved efficiently, all else equal.

### Competitive intensity

A fast-growing business in a commoditizing market may deserve less than growth alone suggests.

## Scenario ranges

Do not hard-code universal multiple bands. Retrieve current public and private comparables when valuation matters.

Then organize scenarios such as:

| Scenario | Typical characteristics |
|---|---|
| Ordinary | slower growth, average retention, modest differentiation |
| Strong | high growth, good margins, strong retention, large runway |
| Exceptional | hypergrowth, category leadership, ecosystem / control point, strategic scarcity |

Apply evidence-based multiple ranges to each.

## Why hypergrowth can look irrational on trailing revenue

Suppose:

```text
January ARR: €10M
June ARR:    €25M
December ARR expectation: €60M
```

A €500M valuation is:

```text
50× January ARR
20× June ARR
8.3× expected December ARR
```

All three statements describe the same valuation.

Therefore always identify whether a reported multiple is based on:

- trailing revenue
- current ARR / run rate
- next-twelve-month revenue
- analyst or investor forward expectations

## Standalone versus strategic acquisition value

Standalone value asks:

> What are this company's future cash flows and market position worth independently?

Strategic acquisition value asks:

> What is owning this asset worth to this particular buyer?

Potential strategic synergies include:

- cloud or infrastructure consumption
- cross-sell into an existing customer base
- protecting an incumbent product
- owning a standard or developer ecosystem
- reducing time-to-market
- acquiring scarce technical talent
- proprietary data or distribution
- preventing a competitor from acquiring the asset

A rational acquirer may pay well above standalone public-market comparables when expected synergies justify it.

## Example: same revenue, different valuation

Two companies each have €50M ARR.

```text
Company A
- 15% growth
- 70% gross margin
- 95% NRR
- crowded market
- weak differentiation

Company B
- 150% growth
- 85% gross margin
- 140% NRR
- category leader
- large market
- ecosystem forming
```

Even with identical current revenue, Company B can command a dramatically higher valuation because its expected future revenue and strategic position are much stronger.

## Valuation workflow

1. Establish current ARR / revenue and its definition.
2. Measure or estimate growth.
3. Assess gross margin and contribution economics.
4. Assess retention and expansion.
5. Estimate remaining market runway.
6. Identify category position and moat.
7. Retrieve current relevant comparables.
8. Build ordinary / strong / exceptional multiple scenarios.
9. Calculate implied valuation ranges.
10. For M&A, add a separate strategic-buyer analysis rather than mixing it into the base case.

## Founder equity is separate

Company valuation does not equal founder wealth.

For founder outcomes, separately model:

```text
Exit value
× founder ownership at exit
- taxes / preferences / other relevant claims
=
approximate founder proceeds
```

Account for dilution from funding rounds, employee option pools, and liquidation preferences when enough information exists.
