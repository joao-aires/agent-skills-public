# Moats and Strategic Control Points

## Principle

Creating customer value and capturing durable economic value are different problems.

A product can be useful, fast-growing, and still be strategically weak if customers can switch easily or a platform can absorb the capability.

## Conventional defensibility

Evaluate relevant sources of durability such as:

- switching costs / workflow lock-in
- proprietary data or accumulated context
- network effects
- ecosystem / marketplace
- distribution advantage
- brand / trust
- standards adoption
- scale economies
- embedded integrations
- regulatory / compliance advantage
- operational complexity customers do not want to own
- unique supply or scarce resources

Do not claim a moat merely because one of these nouns appears. Explain the causal mechanism.

## Accumulating asset

Ask:

> What gets stronger as more customers use the product?

Possible assets:

- data
- workflow history
- integrations
- community
- developer mindshare
- partner ecosystem
- reputation
- operational learning
- installed base
- standards influence

If nothing accumulates, the company may need exceptional distribution or product velocity to remain differentiated.

## Control-point test

Ask:

> If this company became the default layer for the workflow, what valuable downstream activity would pass through it?

Potential control surfaces include:

- identity / authorization
- workflow state
- transaction flow
- distribution
- data access
- policy / governance
- marketplace discovery
- integration routing
- system of record

A control point is valuable when other activity depends on it and bypassing it is meaningfully costly.

## Control versus bottleneck

A temporary bottleneck is not automatically a durable control point.

Test:

- Can standards remove the bottleneck?
- Can customers multi-home?
- Can an incumbent bundle around it?
- Does the control point create value or merely friction?
- Is bypassing technically easy?
- Does regulation force interoperability?

## Commoditization risk

For every proposed moat ask what happens if the underlying technology becomes 10× cheaper or easier.

Strong strategies often move toward complements that become more valuable as the core technology commoditizes.

## Platform dependency

Assess dependency on:

- model providers
- cloud platforms
- app stores
- social networks
- payment rails
- operating systems
- data providers

A company can have a good product moat but poor bargaining power against a critical platform.

## Open-source dynamics

Open source can create developer mindshare, ecosystem, standards influence, and distribution while simultaneously commoditizing the software itself.

The moat may live in:

- managed operations
- enterprise trust
- proprietary control plane
- ecosystem
- data
- distribution
- integration depth

Evaluate adoption advantage separately from monetization defensibility.

## Durability test

For each claimed moat state:

```text
Asset / mechanism:
Why customers care:
Why it strengthens with scale/time:
How competitors attack it:
How customers bypass it:
What future change weakens it:
Confidence:
```

## Strategic implication

The objective is not "have the most moats."

Choose a wedge and expansion sequence that progressively improves bargaining power, retention, distribution, and control over something customers genuinely value.
