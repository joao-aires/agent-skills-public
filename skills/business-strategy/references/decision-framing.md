# Decision Framing

## Purpose

Strategy quality is limited by decision quality. Before gathering large amounts of information, identify the decision the analysis must improve.

## Frame the decision

Capture:

- **Decision** — what choice must be made?
- **Decision owner** — who will act on the answer?
- **Time horizon** — when must the choice work?
- **Scope** — product, segment, geography, company, channel, technology layer, etc.
- **Objective** — what outcome is being optimized?
- **Constraints** — capital, team, regulation, time, distribution, technology, brand, risk tolerance.
- **Options already considered** — including status quo / do nothing.
- **Reversibility** — how expensive is it to change course later?

A vague prompt such as "analyze this market" should be converted into a decision such as:

> Should we enter this market, with which wedge and buyer, during the next 18 months?

## Distinguish decision types

### Opportunity evaluation

Question: Is this worth pursuing?

Key evidence:
- problem intensity
- reachable demand
- economics
- competition
- timing
- defensibility

### Strategic development

Question: What should the business/product become?

Key evidence:
- alternative market configurations
- wedge paths
- market evolution
- control points
- future robustness

### Comparative decision

Question: A, B, C, or none?

Use explicit decision criteria and opportunity cost. Do not independently analyze each option and leave the trade-off implicit.

### Market entry

Question: Where and how should we enter?

Separate:
- target segment
- geography
- channel
- positioning
- product adaptation
- sequencing

### Build / buy / partner / open source

Compare not only cost but strategic ownership, speed, learning, dependency, distribution, ecosystem effects, and future control.

## Decision criteria

Use criteria that actually determine success. Examples:

- customer value
- speed to validation
- distribution advantage
- capital efficiency
- market size
- margin potential
- defensibility
- strategic fit
- regulatory feasibility
- future resilience

Do not use a weighted score merely because it looks rigorous. Scoring is useful for exposing disagreements and trade-offs; final judgment should explain causal reasoning.

## Reversible versus irreversible choices

For reversible choices, prefer cheap learning and speed.

For expensive or path-dependent choices, require more evidence and explicitly analyze lock-in, opportunity cost, and exit options.

## Output

For meaningful decisions, state the frame before the analysis:

```text
Decision:
Objective:
Horizon:
Constraints:
Current options:
What would materially change the choice:
```

If the analysis later reveals that the original decision frame was wrong, update it rather than forcing the evidence into the old frame.
