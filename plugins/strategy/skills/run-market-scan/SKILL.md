# Run a market scan

Establish the market reality a decision depends on: who buys, what they spend, who competes, what is changing and where a real opening exists. Stop at the depth the decision needs and record what remains uncertain.

Use [business strategy](../business-strategy/SKILL.md) as the logic owner. Load [market entry](../business-strategy/references/market-entry.md) for beachheads and competitor profiles, [market system mapping](../business-strategy/references/market-system-mapping.md) for actors and profit pools, [competitive dynamics](../business-strategy/references/competitive-dynamics.md) for the three levels of competition, and [research protocol](../business-strategy/references/research-protocol.md) for evidence discipline. Use [craft-competitive-analysis](../craft-competitive-analysis/SKILL.md) to shape the competitor section.

When the affected stack, architecture or domain has installed expertise or guidance, read it and carry its conclusions. If none exists, use project evidence and primary sources; this activity keeps the outcome, acceptance and stopping point.

## Own the request

Convert the prompt into a question with a decision attached, and state the market definition first: customer, geography, job, product layer, pricing unit and horizon. Choose **light** depth for a bounded question answerable from existing and public evidence, or **deep** for an entry, positioning or strategy decision that needs system, competitor and trend analysis. Say which you are using. Do not inflate a light question into a landscape study.

## Inputs

- the decision, its owner and horizon;
- current strategy or thesis, if one exists;
- available evidence and source access, plus known constraints such as regulation, channel access, capital and geography.

## Work

1. Map the system: users, buyers, beneficiaries, blockers, suppliers, channels, platforms, complements and regulators. Follow the budget and the profit pool, not only the product category.
2. Define segments that share a job, trigger, buying criteria and route to market. Build the competitor set across product competitors, capability owners, whole-vision competitors, the status quo, internal build and open source. Profile only those that can change the decision.
3. For each profiled competitor, establish what they do better, where their model constrains them, their likely response and the evidence date.
4. Scan structural trends and timing — technology, cost, regulation, standards, consolidation, budget migration and behaviour change — separating fact, reported estimate and inference. Size the market only where it changes the decision, bottom-up and tied to real buyers or usage.
5. State the opening precisely: unserved, underserved, structurally abandoned or temporarily ignored. A missing feature is not an opening. End with the implication for wedge, positioning, entry mode or durability, and the gaps that could reverse it.

## Output

- market definition and depth;
- actors, value chain, budgets and profit pools;
- decision-relevant competitor profiles with evidence dates;
- trends, opening assessment and strategic implication;
- evidence limits, contradictions and open questions.

## Stop and hand off

Stop at the market and competitive implication. Do not write the positioning statement, choose the strategy, design the model or commit to entry. Hand positioning to [define-positioning](../define-positioning/SKILL.md), business-model and entry-structure choices to [design-business-model](../design-business-model/SKILL.md), the strategy decision to [set-strategy](../set-strategy/SKILL.md), and testable market claims to [test-assumptions](../test-assumptions/SKILL.md).
