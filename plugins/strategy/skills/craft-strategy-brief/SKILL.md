# Strategy brief guide

A strategy brief is the durable statement of what the business has chosen and why. It should be readable in one sitting, specific enough to guide product and commercial decisions, and honest about what would change it.

Start from the [strategy brief](../business-strategy/assets/strategy-brief.md) template. The method lives in [choice cascade and strategy brief](../business-strategy/references/choice-cascade-and-strategy-brief.md); this guide owns the craft of the record itself.

This is a local strategy record, not an artifact type in the SDLC artifact system. If the project keeps its strategy in an existing brief, decision log or strategy state, adapt there rather than creating a parallel authority.

## Reader and job

Write for the decision owner and the next operator: someone who must act on the choices, and a skeptic who must be able to test them. The brief is not a pitch, a status report or a plan.

Lead with the decision and its status. State whether the strategy is proposed, accepted or superseded, who owns the decision and when it is reviewed. A polished brief with no status invites the reader to assume a commitment that was never made.

## What the brief must carry

- **Diagnosis.** The critical challenge or obstacle, supported by evidence, and why it matters now. A goal is not a diagnosis.
- **The integrated choices.** Winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities and management systems — or the Rumelt kernel when the strategy is challenge-led. Each choice must support the others.
- **Alternatives and opportunity cost.** The materially different options considered, why each lost, what the chosen option forecloses and what that costs.
- **Economics.** Payer, pricing metric, market size and obtainability, unit economics and capital intensity, stated consistently with the chosen segment and motion.
- **Durability.** What accumulates, what creates switching or bargaining power, what can be bundled or commoditized, and why the position survives a mature market.
- **Assumptions and falsifiers.** The critical beliefs, their confidence and evidence grade, and the observable result, threshold, window and decision each would reopen.
- **Tripwires and review.** The signals that trigger a review, their owners and dates, and the cadence that keeps the strategy current.
- **Decision history and evidence.** Material reversals, the evidence that caused them, and links to the analysis — kept compact, without turning the brief into a bibliography.

## Keep it decision-dense

Use one page for a bounded choice and three at most for a full strategy. Prefer a table or a short causal paragraph over a section per framework. Every paragraph should help a reader decide, execute or challenge. Delete headings that carry no decision.

State evidence as fact, derived calculation, inference, assumption or judgment. Where a claim is unproven, write the test rather than smoothing the prose. Where two choices conflict, show the conflict instead of hiding it behind neutral language.

## Review questions

1. Can the reader state the strategy in five sentences without the document?
2. Does each choice support the aspiration, the where-to-play and the economics?
3. Are the exclusions real, with reasons, or is everything still in scope?
4. Does every critical assumption have a falsifier with a threshold and window?
5. Is the opportunity cost named, including the best rejected alternative?
6. Is the status honest, and is the decision owner's acceptance recorded?
7. Would a competitor or skeptic know what to attack first?
8. Is any part of this actually a PRD, a roadmap, a status update or a pitch?

## Common failures

- A cascade where how-to-win restates the product.
- An aspiration with no customer, horizon or definition of winning.
- Where-to-play lists that include every segment.
- Capabilities named but not funded.
- Alternatives described but only one analysed.
- Exclusions omitted to avoid conflict.
- Falsifiers that can never fire.
- Economics quoted without assumptions or evidence grade.
- A brief that quietly becomes a PRD, roadmap or pitch deck.

## Boundaries

The brief owns business choices. Product requirements, product roadmaps, engineering change records and release plans have their own owners outside this package. A pitch deck or summary is a projection of the brief for one audience and must not become the authority.
