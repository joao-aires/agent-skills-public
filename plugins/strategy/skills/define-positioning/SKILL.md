# Define positioning

Make the choice obvious relative to the alternatives the customer already understands: for whom, against what, with what magnitude of difference and at what switching cost.

Use [business strategy](../business-strategy/SKILL.md) as the logic owner. Apply [positioning and category](../business-strategy/references/positioning-and-category.md) and [value proposition](../business-strategy/references/value-proposition.md); use [competitive dynamics](../business-strategy/references/competitive-dynamics.md) and [craft-competitive-analysis](../craft-competitive-analysis/SKILL.md) for the comparison set.

## Own the request

Frame the decision: which customer, comparison set, category frame and value claim should govern product, pricing and sales choices. Positioning is a choice about what the offer is and is not.

## Inputs

- customer and discovery evidence, in the customer's language;
- the real alternatives, including doing nothing, manual work, internal build and bundled capability;
- current capability, buying criteria, proof available today and proof still missing.

## Work

1. Identify the competitive alternative: what the customer would use or do if this offer did not exist. Treat the status quo as the strongest competitor.
2. Define the best-fit customer where the advantage is unusually acute: circumstance, trigger, job, current alternative, cost of the status quo and buying path. Do not average across segments.
3. Classify attributes as unique, better, table stakes or unproven claim, replacing untestable adjectives with a mechanism. Translate capability into customer consequence, state who captures the value and who pays, and classify the magnitude as incremental, meaningfully better, dramatically better or qualitatively new.
4. Choose the category frame and say what it invites the buyer to compare against, which budget it attaches to and what price comparison it risks.
5. State the proof route for each material claim, the differentiation that changes the comparison, the remaining switching anxieties with their counter-evidence, and the explicit exclusions and behaviour that would falsify the proposition.

## Output

- positioning statement as a compact argument;
- best-fit customer and excluded segments;
- alternatives and category frame with their economic consequences;
- value claim, magnitude, proof status and proof gaps;
- switching anxieties, exclusions and the test that would falsify the claim.

## Stop and hand off

Stop at the positioning argument. Do not produce campaign copy, a brand system or a pricing decision here. Hand pricing and packaging implications to [design-business-model](../design-business-model/SKILL.md), reach and launch to [plan-go-to-market](../plan-go-to-market/SKILL.md), proof tests to [test-assumptions](../test-assumptions/SKILL.md), and an integrated strategy choice to [set-strategy](../set-strategy/SKILL.md).
