# Discover customers

Replace the team's model of the customer with observed reality: the trigger, the current workflow, its cost, who pays and what would have to change. Stop at an evidence-backed customer model and its sample limits.

Use [business strategy](../business-strategy/SKILL.md) as the logic owner. Apply [customer discovery](../business-strategy/references/customer-discovery.md) and [customer behavior and culture](../business-strategy/references/customer-behavior-and-culture.md). Use the [interview guide](../business-strategy/assets/interview-guide.md) to keep sessions comparable.

When the affected stack, architecture or domain has installed guidance, read it and carry its conclusions. If none exists, use project evidence and primary sources; this activity keeps the outcome, acceptance and stopping point.

## Own the request

State the decision this discovery can change — segment, offer, wedge, first slice or whether to keep investing — and the assumption it tests. Discovery that cannot change a decision is conversation.

## Inputs

- the customer hypothesis: role, circumstance, trigger, job and current alternative;
- access to real participants and permission to contact or record them, plus existing research, support themes, sales notes and usage data, so the work does not repeat them.

## Work

1. Turn the hypothesis into a testable ICP: role and circumstance, trigger, job, status quo, cost of that status quo, budget owner, buying process and reachability. Distinguish user, economic buyer, beneficiary and blocker.
2. Recruit across more than one route, and state who was reachable, who was excluded and how that biases the sample.
3. Run behavioral interviews that reconstruct the last real occurrence: sequence, tools, handoffs, delays, rework, cost and what was tried and abandoned. Do not pitch, lead with the solution or ask hypotheticals.
4. Map the status quo and the forces around change — push, pull, switching anxiety and habit — and record the customer's exact words.
5. Rank signals on the commitment ladder, keeping interest, access, manual effort, paid commitment and renewal separate. Keep a compact discovery ledger with disconfirming evidence, then state the evidence limits: sample, selection, recall, social desirability, insider knowledge and timing.

Do not invent personas, quotes, segments, percentages or willingness-to-pay claims. Do not convert discovery into market size or retention evidence.

## Output

- the ICP and the roles inside it, revised against observation;
- the current workflow and its measured or estimated cost;
- the job, trigger and switching anxieties that matter;
- strongest behavioral evidence, its limits, what remains unknown, and the assumptions changed with the next evidence that would alter the decision.

## Stop and hand off

Stop when the customer reality is sharp enough to change a segment, offer or decision. Hand positioning to [define-positioning](../define-positioning/SKILL.md), demand tests to [test-assumptions](../test-assumptions/SKILL.md), and pricing or model questions to [design-business-model](../design-business-model/SKILL.md). Return changed assumptions to [validate-idea](../validate-idea/SKILL.md) or [set-strategy](../set-strategy/SKILL.md).
