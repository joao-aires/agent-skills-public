# Model finances

Expose the assumptions that drive an outcome instead of presenting one impressive number. Stop at a model with its drivers, scenarios and sensitivity made explicit. A model is a set of arguments, not a forecast.

Use [business strategy](../business-strategy/SKILL.md) as the logic owner. Apply [financial scenario modeling](../business-strategy/references/financial-scenario-modeling.md) and [revenue quality and unit economics](../business-strategy/references/revenue-quality-and-unit-economics.md). Tie pricing to [pricing and packaging](../business-strategy/references/pricing-and-packaging.md), market size to [market sizing](../business-strategy/references/market-sizing.md), and record per-segment economics with the [unit economics record](../business-strategy/assets/unit-economics-record.md).

When the affected stack, architecture or domain has installed expertise or guidance, read it and carry its conclusions. If none exists, use project evidence and primary sources; this activity keeps the outcome, acceptance and stopping point.

## Own the request

Frame the decision the model must inform: affordability, runway, a pricing change, a hiring plan, a scenario comparison or what must be true for a target. Say which decisions the model does not settle.

## Inputs

- the accepted or proposed business model and pricing;
- current actuals, cohort data and definitions;
- capacity constraints, cost structure, cash, burn, payment terms and obligations.

## Work

1. Choose the revenue build that matches the business: customers × ACV, segmented, usage × price, transaction take rate or a multi-year recurring model. Show the drivers, not only the output, and constrain growth with sales productivity, cycle length, onboarding, channel reach, market maturity and implementation load.
2. Model cost of revenue and gross margin including every material variable cost. Do not treat pass-through infrastructure or inference spend as high-margin software.
3. Model retention and expansion from cohorts, tying ACV expansion to a mechanism rather than a mechanical annual uplift.
4. Build conservative, base and upside scenarios, plus at least one strategic downside where an assumption fails — bundling, weak conversion, price compression or behaviour shift. Run sensitivity on the two or three variables that most change the outcome.
5. Work backwards from any target to what must be true, then test market size, acquisition capacity and retention against it. State runway, the month the next raise must close, and how the model reconciles to accounting and the bank. Keep the original forecast visible when revising.

## Output

- revenue build and its drivers;
- cost structure, contribution and gross margin;
- retention and expansion assumptions;
- scenario table with assumptions, sensitivities and what-must-be-true analysis;
- runway, cash timing, limits and what would change the conclusion.

## Stop and hand off

Stop at the decision the model informs. Do not issue a valuation opinion, run a fundraise or set live prices. Hand capital sizing and process to [raise-capital](../raise-capital/SKILL.md), pricing and packaging choices to [design-business-model](../design-business-model/SKILL.md), and capacity implications to [build-strategic-roadmap](../build-strategic-roadmap/SKILL.md).
