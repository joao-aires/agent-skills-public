# Design a business model

Turn customer value into a commercially viable business: a payer, a price metric, a package, economics that survive and a reason the position persists. Stop at a model with its assumptions and the evidence that would confirm or refute them.

Use [business strategy](../business-strategy/SKILL.md) as the logic owner. Apply [pricing and packaging](../business-strategy/references/pricing-and-packaging.md), [revenue quality and unit economics](../business-strategy/references/revenue-quality-and-unit-economics.md), [economic power and value capture](../business-strategy/references/economic-power-and-value-capture.md) and [moats and control points](../business-strategy/references/moats-and-control-points.md). Record economics with the [unit economics record](../business-strategy/assets/unit-economics-record.md).

When the affected stack, architecture or domain has installed guidance, read it and carry its conclusions. If none exists, use project evidence and primary sources; this activity keeps the outcome, acceptance and stopping point.

## Own the request

Frame the decision: which business model, pricing metric and packaging should the chosen segment and wedge use, and what evidence would show the economics work or fail. The model is a strategic choice, not a downstream calculation.

## Inputs

- the chosen customer, buyer, wedge and value proposition;
- willingness-to-pay evidence and the cost of the real alternative;
- delivery cost structure including variable, infrastructure, support and AI costs, plus market size, capital intensity and the return the business must produce.

## Work

1. Compare materially different monetization models when plausible — subscription, usage, transaction, marketplace, services, licensing, open-source/commercial or hybrid — and say whether each changes the buyer, product, channel or adoption path.
2. Set price against the customer's alternative and the value of the difference, not cost-plus or a competitor's list price. State the value split and what the customer retains after switching cost.
3. Choose the pricing metric and packaging by job and outcome. Check alignment, predictability, controllability, measurability, gaming and margin protection.
4. Compute contribution, gross margin, CAC payback and retention by segment, not only in aggregate, and tie market size to an obtainable revenue path constrained by reach and sales capacity.
5. Test durability: what accumulates, what creates switching or bargaining power, what can be bundled or commoditized, and why the position survives a mature market. Name the critical assumptions, their evidence grade and the cheapest test that would change the model.

## Output

- chosen model, payer and pricing metric with rationale;
- packaging, entry offer and expansion path;
- unit economics by segment with assumptions and sensitivities;
- market size and obtainable revenue tied to the model;
- durability thesis, value leakage, assumptions and what would reopen the model.

## Stop and hand off

Stop at the model and its economics. Do not build the full multi-year financial model, launch the motion or change live prices. Hand the driver-based model and runway to [model-finances](../model-finances/SKILL.md), reach and launch to [plan-go-to-market](../plan-go-to-market/SKILL.md), willingness-to-pay tests to [test-assumptions](../test-assumptions/SKILL.md), and the integrated choice to [set-strategy](../set-strategy/SKILL.md).
