# Control and evidence reasoning

Use when mapping the actual service to scoped criteria and examination needs. Official criteria and the auditor's agreed engagement determine the assessment; this guide supplies engineering questions rather than copied control requirements.

| Control concern | Design questions | Useful evidence distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access lifecycle | Who grants, reviews, changes and revokes access across users, operators and services? | Configured roles versus actual approved grants, reviews and timely revocations |
| Changes and delivery | How are changes reviewed, verified, identified and promoted/recovered? | Pipeline definition versus performed checks/approvals and deployed artifact identity |
| Detection and response | What events require action and who responds? | Alert configuration versus observed response, resolution and exception handling |
| Recovery | What service/data loss commitments apply and how are dependencies restored? | Backup success versus exercised restore and actual recovery evidence |
| Vendors and shared responsibilities | What relies on providers and what remains with this service/customer? | Provider attestation scope/period versus implemented complementary controls |
| Risk and exceptions | Who evaluates changed exposure and follows through on accepted treatment? | Written policy versus actual decisions, exceptions and remediation evidence |

An evidence item should support a specific assertion about a control, population and period. Record actual source/collection date and protect sensitive data. A screenshot may support a configuration observation but rarely establishes an entire period of control operation. Report coverage and sampling limits; agree audit sampling and sufficiency with the auditor rather than inventing them.

Use actual service records for policies, ownership and evidence links. No fixed document suite, tracker, compliance vendor or certification claim is implied.
